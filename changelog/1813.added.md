Add `wp.JetSpace()` and `wp.JetSpace2()`, forward-mode jet types that carry a value together with its derivatives.
Evaluating an ordinary `@wp.func` over them yields its gradient, or its gradient and dense Hessian, with no
hand-written derivatives. The number of derivative directions is fixed when the types are created, so these suit local
derivatives of one element rather than differentiation with respect to an unbounded number of variables. See
`warp/examples/optim/example_implicit_projection.py`.
